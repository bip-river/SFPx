# Regression checks

`npm run check` parses the JavaScript files, resolves and validates the actual demo catalog, and confirms every CSS custom property used in `styles.css` is defined.

`npm test` runs Node's test runner with jsdom. The DOM tests load the real ES module graph using VM modules (Node prints an experimental VM-module warning). They exercise the real markup, events, catalog loader, validation, and checkout code. They cover:

- A start page on a first visit or an unknown fragment, "Start now" leading to step 1, and step 1's Back link returning to it.
- One screen at a time; Continue, the Back link and the browser Back button moving between screens; focus and page titles following the screen; later screens unreachable before their earlier answers, including through history.
- Paid and no-fee permits, repeat-Pay identity after going back, and changed-order identity.
- PDF invalidation when purchaser details, quantity or the agreement change, and the finished-permit screen becoming unreachable.
- Change links on the check screen, with Continue returning there once the changed answers are complete.
- Reset ("Start over" and "Start another permit") of purchaser fields, saved selections, old transaction references, and payloads.
- Active-ID restoration with expired products ahead of a selection, a reload returning to its screen, and purchaser details always reentered; missing, expired, and index-only selections.
- A new area clearing the permit choice.
- Quantity bounds and increments, the stepper staying within limits without dropping focus, eligibility independent of display names and certified inside the chosen permit before details are entered, and open-tab sale expiry.
- Malformed catalog data, malformed saved state, blocked storage, and fetch failures.
- URL normalization and script-protocol rejection.
- Relative catalog dates (`today±N`), malformed relative dates failing closed, and no shipped product sale ending for future visitors through 2040.
- Areas listed only when the chosen collection type has a permit on sale or coming soon, a message when a state has none, coming-soon products listed separately and not selectable, the validity shown on screen matching the demo PDF, eligibility certification carried for paid products with a rule, the Privacy Act notice on the details screen with no checkbox, an optional middle name carried into the summary, payload and permit, and production-only email promises labelled "In production:".
- Changing the collection type keeps the state, and keeps the area when it offers the new type.
- Confirm-email errors that follow delivery-email edits, checkout IDs without `crypto.randomUUID` (plain-HTTP device testing), short permit names described by price and limits, quantity guidance after restore, and validity day counts across a DST change.
- Field errors that appear once a field is left and clear while typing, Continue buttons that open a linked error summary (radio groups link to their first option), and blur-time errors held back while Continue is pressed.
- Failure paths, each with its exact visitor-facing message: a catalog that returns an HTTP error, invalid JSON, invalid data or never answers (timed out after 15 seconds), staying on every screen with a "Try again" button that recovers without a reload; Continue while the catalog is still loading; a PDF engine or font that fails to download, keeping entries and working on retry; characters the permit cannot print, caught when the field is left and, as a backstop, linked from the PDF error back to the field on the details screen; an oversized checkout payload; and uncaught errors from the page's own scripts (other origins' are ignored).
- Every purchaser field message, in order: missing, unprintable, too short, too long.
- Paying through Pay.ure. The DOM tests follow the visitor to Pay.ure and back as separate page loads in one tab (`tests/dom.mjs` records navigation and carries sessionStorage across): the collection Pay.ure is given (no purchaser details), the handoff removed on return, the payment in the payload, permit card note and PDF, and "Get my permit" without a second payment after going back. They also cover a cancel, the browser Back button from Pay.ure (reload and back-forward cache), a return Pay.ure has no payment for, a return without the checkout, blocked storage, Start over, and entries checked again before leaving; the checks on return in the payload; Pay.ure being down; a payment for the wrong amount refused with no permit and nothing remembered; a paid permit warned about when the same details are entered again (after Start over, which keeps the records) or Pay.ure's Return link is used, with no reference numbers shown and no purchaser details stored; getting it again with either reference (typed loosely, Enter not paying), a wrong reference refused, five locking the field; a loose match (same permit and email) warned without a way to get it again; the paid heading and receipt note; and a failure after payment saying first that the payment went through.
- Demo tools (`tests/demo.test.js`): off until the footer button turns them on, staying on across page loads; the drawer listing only what applies to each screen (and switches that are on); fills showing each field's own message, including the choice screens; Go to answering every earlier step; a switch breaking until switched off with the banner on every page and Fix all; the catalog switch reloading and failing each chosen way; the storage, Pay.ure down, wrong amount and lost-checkout switches whatever the card; Pay.ure's test numbers and fills appearing only for the chosen method on step 1; Reset; and the stored-data view.
- Pay.ure on its own (`tests/payure.test.js`): no payment in progress, the payment step, every card and bank field message, each decline and insufficient-funds message with the attempt recorded and another method then paying, the outage page, the wrong-amount card, the Use buttons, the review step and Change, authorization, a single return per payment, only the last four digits stored, cancelling, finished payments that cannot be paid again, the collection store's rules, and the test numbers themselves.
- Catalog validation listing every problem with its location, rule and value, and the browser character list matching the embedded PDF font.

`npm run test:browser` uses Chromium by default and runs two files. Set `BROWSER=firefox` or `BROWSER=webkit` for the other Playwright engines. At 1280px and 390px, the tests start from the start page, choose a collection type with the keyboard, move through every screen, check that focus lands on each new question, enter an invalid quantity without focus being taken, follow an error-summary link, go to Pay.ure and cancel, go again and pay with a test card (with an error summary, an expired date and Enter to continue), use the browser Back button to reach Pay.ure's "You've already paid" page, follow its Return button to the already-paid notice, enter the same details, see the earlier-payment warning and get the same permit again with its permit number, reload with purchaser reentry, get a no-fee permit, download both Unicode PDFs, start another permit, and check for horizontal overflow. Axe scans check WCAG 2 A/AA, 2.1 AA and 2.2 AA on every screen, on the error summary, on Pay.ure's payment step, card errors, review step and already-paid page, and on the already-paid notice; the failure-path file adds Pay.ure's decline and outage pages and the wrong-amount refusal. All eight smoke-page checks must pass. `CHROMIUM_EXECUTABLE` can point to an existing Chromium executable.

`tests/browser-errors.mjs` then breaks one thing per scenario the way a network, server or browser would and checks the exact message shown, what the visitor can do next, and an axe scan of the error state: `products.json` returning 503, invalid JSON, a dropped connection, and no answer at all (with Playwright's clock run past the timeout); the demo tools' catalog switch reloading to break and Fix all reloading to mend (with axe scans of the drawer, banner and fills), the demo tools as a phone bottom sheet on Pay.ure's two steps, `vendor/pdfmake.js` failing to download after paying on Pay.ure and then succeeding on retry without a second payment, a declined card followed by one that pays, Pay.ure being down, a payment for the wrong amount refused on return, and `vendor/permit-font.js` returning 500; emoji and CJK characters in purchaser fields; `app.js` missing, a module it imports missing, and `app.js` throwing while starting; and JavaScript turned off. A browser remembers a module that failed to download, so the PDF retry scenario checks that a second attempt really fetches again.

`npm run test:safari` runs Selenium against installed Safari on macOS. Enable Safari automation first (`sudo safaridriver --enable`). This tests Safari itself; the separate WebKit job tests Playwright's WebKit build on Linux. Safari coverage includes the start page and every screen, Enter in the quantity field, both checkout paths, PDF generation, focus on each new screen and on the finished permit, the browser Back button, reload, starting another permit, and the same automated accessibility rules. Actual PDF downloading is covered by the Playwright jobs.

GitHub Actions runs syntax/catalog checks, logic/DOM/PDF tests, Chromium/Firefox/WebKit jobs, and an installed-Safari job on macOS. A passing automated scan does not substitute for a person listening with a screen reader; see [the manual checks](accessibility.md).

## PDF checks

The package tests check the page order (permit, stipulations, each tag sheet followed by a sheet of load records, maps, area notices, reference), that every sheet's load records match its tags in number and sit behind them for a long-edge or short-edge turn (checked from text positions in the PDF), that tag sheets start on the front of a sheet whatever the permit's length (the one-page map fills any gap, so the package has no blank pages), one tag per half cord or tree with its number, amount and expiry on each, packages without tags, the footer with the permit number on every page, and that the committed sample documents still match their content. Rule tests cover the haul tag cascade, duration ranges, office ID format and permit numbers.

The PDF tests extract Unicode text and check text coordinates on every page. They cover accented Latin and Navajo characters, Greek and Cyrillic names, decomposed accents, long unbroken strings, multi-page content, page numbers, unsupported glyph errors, and complete document tails. DOM tests also cover Reset during asynchronous generation, correction/retry after an error, select change events without input events, and back-forward cache purchaser-field clearing, and unchanged blur/change events preserving the prepared permit and button text nodes. Quantity edits discard the prepared permit. PDF development verification uses Poppler rendering and visual inspection in addition to text extraction.

The embedded DejaVu Sans font has finite character coverage. Latin, Greek, Cyrillic and the tested Navajo characters are supported; this is not a promise of every Unicode script or emoji. Missing glyphs cause an explicit error rather than silent replacement squares. The PDF remains a demo, and no PDF/UA or tagged-document accessibility certification is claimed.

## Checkout identity and demo limits

An unchanged checkout snapshot reuses its reference, including after going back from the finished permit and paying again. Editing selections, quantities, purchaser values, or the agreement invalidates the prepared permit, and the finished-permit screen can no longer be reached. Generation is asynchronous: the Pay button announces busy status, failures retain entries for retry, and an outdated result cannot appear after Reset or edits. Transaction snapshots and purchaser information stay in memory; reload requires purchaser reentry and creates a new checkout identity.

These checks do not implement or validate a real payment processor, CBS, permit issuance, email, or server-side idempotency.
